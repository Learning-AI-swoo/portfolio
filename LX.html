How I Think

Three principles

Protect the friction that helps learning

I pay close attention to the difference between productive effort and avoidable friction. Good learning often requires challenge, repetition, uncertainty, and real cognitive work. I do not try to remove that.

What I try to remove is the friction that does not help. Unclear instructions, weak course structure, scattered information, clumsy workflows, and poorly designed handoffs can drain time and energy without improving learning or performance.

This idea shapes how I design courses, support faculty, structure training, and build systems. The goal is to make room for the effort that matters.

Define the real problem before choosing a tool

I do not assume that every problem needs a technical answer. Some problems are structural. Some are instructional. Some come from policy, culture, or coordination gaps. The tool only helps once the real problem is clear.

That usually means slowing down at the beginning, asking what is actually breaking down, and choosing a response that fits the situation rather than forcing a preferred solution.

Build for reuse, clarity, and capacity

I prefer systems that keep helping after the first implementation. That can mean reusable templates, clearer logic, better documentation, stronger training, or workflows that other people can actually sustain.

The goal is not only to solve the immediate problem. It is to leave the organization better able to handle similar problems the next time.

Course Development & Async Delivery

Building learning that works without you in the room

Most of my course development work starts with a real operational need: a required course students aren't engaging with, an SME whose knowledge is locked in lecture slides, a training sequence that doesn't scale. I work from problem to design to deployment — coordinating SMEs, managing production, and building LMS infrastructure that runs reliably after launch.

WM301: ILT to Fully Asynchronous Course Conversion

A required Western Medicine course structured as synchronous lecture sessions — students questioned its value, attendance was inconsistent, and the format didn't support flexible scheduling. I led the full redesign and conversion to a fully asynchronous format.

Collaborated with faculty SME to restructure lecture content into 10–20 min chunked video segments. Coordinated recording, video editing, branding, and LMS build. Designed case-based learning activities for each unit: patient scenario discussions, lab interpretation exercises, spirometry practice. Built Kirkpatrick-aligned assessments and embedded AI-assisted discussion prompts throughout. Added a course support chatbot for async Q&A. Structured RSI compliance into the course architecture. Launched Spring 2025 — positive reception and adopted as the blueprint for future async conversions.

AI Champion Micro-Credential — Competency-Based Program Design

A short-form, stackable credential for faculty and staff demonstrating practical AI competency — not passive awareness, but demonstrated workflow integration. Designed with performance-based assessment at every tier.

Three competency levels: AI-Curious (understands what AI can and cannot do), AI-Practitioner (uses AI tools in actual workflow, can show results), AI-Champion (can train others, has redesigned workflow around AI). Prerequisite onboarding built in Articulate Rise as a self-paced 30-minute module integrating Tabs, Sorting Activity, Process blocks, and embedded case study. Assessment is portfolio-based: participants demonstrate what they built, not what they read. Pilot cohort planned through CTLE.

AI Integration & Educational Innovation

Pioneering AI in healthcare education

When AI tools became ubiquitous, our institution had the same gap most did: students were already using these tools, but there was no policy, no guidance, and no structured way to build actual competency. I approached it as a design problem with three layers: institutional policy, faculty readiness, and student skill.

AI Literacy for Healthcare Professionals

Designed a 10-week blended course built around a structured AI literacy framework. Students don't learn to use AI passively. They learn to question it, stress-test it, and decide when not to use it.

6 synchronous Zoom sessions + 4 async weeks. Pre/post surveys aligned to Kirkpatrick Levels 1–3. Key activities: red team testing (deliberately breaking AI), custom assistant builds, personal ethics frameworks.
